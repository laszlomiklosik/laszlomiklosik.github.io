Alternate Tunings

Standard tuning (E A D G B E) is just the starting point. Alternate tunings open up entirely new voicings, resonances, and sonic landscapes — many rooted in centuries-old traditions.

Open G
D G D G B D

Beloved by blues and rock slide players. The strings form a G major chord open. Keith Richards famously removed the low E string and played Open G for much of the Rolling Stones' catalogue. Robert Johnson also used this tuning.

♪ Brown Sugar, Little Red Rooster
Open D
D A D F♯ A D

Another classic slide and bottleneck tuning, forming a D major chord open. Richie Havens used it for his powerful strummed style — including the iconic Woodstock performance of "Freedom." Joni Mitchell also explored it extensively.

♪ Freedom (Havens), Slide Blues
DADGAD
D A D G A D

Discovered by Davy Graham after encountering Moroccan musicians in Tangiers, DADGAD creates a suspended, modal sound that fits both Celtic fingerstyle and Middle Eastern music perfectly. Jimmy Page used it for "Kashmir." Pierre Bensusan is its modern virtuoso.

♪ Kashmir, She Moved Through the Fair
Drop D
D A D G B E

The most popular alternate tuning — only the low E is dropped to D. This enables powerful, open-fifth power chords on the bottom three strings and a thunderous bass note. Used extensively in folk (Neil Young), acoustic rock, and metal.

♪ Harvest Moon, Heart of Gold
Open E
E B E G♯ B E

Open E produces a bright, resonant E major chord and is a favourite of electric blues and acoustic slide players. Duane Allman and Derek Trucks used it extensively. Because it raises several strings, it places extra tension on the neck — some players tune down to Open D and use a capo.

♪ Layla (slide parts), Little Wing
Open A
E A E A C♯ E

Open A produces a bright, punchy A major chord. It's closely related to Open G (just a step up) and similarly beloved for slide and blues. Mississippi Fred McDowell and Ry Cooder have used this tuning to great effect.

♪ Shake 'Em On Down, Motherless Children
Open C
C G C G C E

A deep, sonorous tuning that produces a rich, full C major chord and enables extensive use of open strings as drones. John Butler (of the John Butler Trio) uses a variation of this tuning to spectacular effect in his fingerstyle guitar work.

♪ Ocean (John Butler), Open C pieces
Double Drop D
D A D G B D

Both E strings are dropped to D, creating a warm, folk-friendly resonance. Neil Young used Double Drop D for "Cinnamon Girl," where the two D strings create a droning, hypnotic quality. Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young also explored this tonality.

♪ Cinnamon Girl, The Needle and the Damage Done

Artist Signature & Special Tunings

Beyond the classic open and modal tunings, individual artists have made certain tunings or approaches entirely their own — sometimes borrowing from tradition, sometimes inventing something new.

Eb Standard
E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B♭ E♭

Every string tuned a half step below standard. Jimi Hendrix and Stevie Ray Vaughan both famously used Eb standard — it eases string bending, allows heavier gauges, and imparts a slightly darker, more vocal tone. John Mayer regularly employs it for the same expressive reasons, particularly in his blues and soul-influenced work.

♪ Voodoo Child, Pride and Joy, Gravity
Open E Minor
E B E G B E

Like Open E but with a minor third — the third string stays at G rather than rising to G♯. This creates a haunting, unresolved quality. John Mayer uses a variant of this open minor approach for "Neon," where the open strings ring against intricate bass lines, blending melody, rhythm and bass into a single guitar part.

♪ Neon, open-minor blues pieces
Chris Martin's Capo Tuning
E A D G B E + capo

Chris Martin of Coldplay rarely strays far from standard tuning, but his signature approach pairs a high capo (often frets 4–7) with specific chord shapes to create the shimmering, piano-like guitar textures heard throughout Coldplay's catalogue. "Yellow" (capo 4) and "The Scientist" show how this simple device transforms standard voicings into something otherworldly.

♪ Yellow, The Scientist, Fix You
Joni Mitchell's Modal Tunings
Various — e.g. C G D F C E

Joni Mitchell used over 50 different tunings in her career, treating the guitar almost as a re-invented instrument for each song. She developed her own notation system to keep track of them. Tunings like CGDFCE ("A Case of You") and DADGBE gave her chord voicings that no standard-tuning player could replicate, enabling her complex jazz-influenced harmonies and intimate fingerstyle work.

♪ A Case of You, Big Yellow Taxi, The Hissing of Summer Lawns
Nick Drake's Altered Tunings
Various — e.g. C G C G C E

Nick Drake composed almost entirely in obscure alternate tunings, giving his music an intimacy that is nearly impossible to replicate in standard. Tunings like CGCGCE ("Pink Moon") and CGCFCE allowed him to use open strings as constant drones beneath delicate, jazz-influenced melodies. His tab was largely a mystery during his lifetime; uncovering his tunings became a pursuit for generations of guitarists.

♪ Pink Moon, River Man, Cello Song
Jacob Collier's Microtonal Approach
Just intonation / custom

Jacob Collier goes beyond conventional equal temperament entirely. He builds and adapts instruments to play in just intonation — where intervals are tuned to pure mathematical ratios rather than the compromises of standard tuning. His self-built "Harmonizer" guitar and multi-neck rigs can produce quarter-tones and chords impossible on a normally fretted guitar, enabling the "negative harmony" re-harmonisations that made him famous on YouTube before his Grammy-winning career.

♪ In My Room, Hideaway, All I Need
